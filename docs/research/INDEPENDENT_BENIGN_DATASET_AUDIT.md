## 1. Executive Summary & Research Motivation

The central scientific hypothesis of the ETTH project is:
> *Does TLS fingerprint information (JA3 and JA4), combined with encrypted-flow behavioral features, improve malicious encrypted traffic detection compared with flow features alone, without TLS payload decryption?*

In the existing Phase 8–10 empirical benchmarks (Corpus v3, 577,238 flows, 80,651 TLS handshakes), **Generalisation-Grade Sufficiency (Level C) is NOT MET**. Specifically:
1. **Zero Cross-Class Fingerprint Overlap:** There are 90 unique JA3 values (46 malicious-only, 44 benign-only) and 82 unique JA4 values (39 malicious-only, 43 benign-only). Overlap between malicious and benign classes is **0.0%**.
2. **Environment & Stack Confounding:** The benign corpus consists entirely of DS-011 (Stratosphere CTU-Normal 2017 captures), generated by Iceweasel/Firefox instances running in a Debian Linux virtualized laboratory. In contrast, malicious traffic originates from Windows virtual machines running banking trojans (Dridex, TrickBot, Emotet) and exploit kit landings (MTA / DS-008).
3. **TLS Version Bias:** The benign corpus is **100.0% TLS 1.2**, whereas modern malicious samples (DS-008) feature TLS 1.3 handshakes.
4. **Source Holdout Collapse:** Under rigorous source-holdout cross-validation (`GroupKFold` by capture environment), models trained on JA3/JA4 alone collapse to near-random performance when tested on unseen network environments. Classifiers memorize environment-specific client stacks rather than malicious behavior.

To resolve the research question definitively, an **independent, diverse, modern benign dataset** must be audited, admitted, and integrated into the benchmark corpus.

## 2. Candidate Dataset In-Depth Audit

We audited five candidate sources offering benign encrypted traffic across six evaluation dimensions:
- **Licensing & Legal Usability** (commercial vs. academic vs. unrestricted open data)
- **Capture Methodology & Realism** (real enterprise/backbone vs. lab host vs. synthetic generator)
- **TLS Version Distribution** (TLS 1.2 vs. TLS 1.3 representation)
- **Handshake Completeness** (untruncated ClientHello packets enabling full JA3/JA4/JA4 v2 hashing)
- **PII & Privacy Exposure** (GDPR/privacy risk, IP anonymization, payload content)
- **Integrity & Reproducibility** (immutable archive availability, published SHA-256 checksums)

---

### Candidate 1: CESNET-TLS22 (CESNET-QUIC22)

- **Provider:** CESNET (Czech National Research and Educational Network) & Masaryk University
- **Repository URL:** `https://zenodo.org/record/7113110` / `https://dataset.cesnet.cz/`
- **Year:** 2022–2023
- **Capture Methodology:** Real-world traffic captured directly on 100 Gbps Czech NREN backbone links connecting research universities, government facilities, and general academic networks. Aggregated into flow-level records with complete cryptographic handshake metadata and packet size/IAT sequences.
- **Licensing:** Creative Commons Attribution-NonCommercial 4.0 International (CC BY-NC 4.0). Completely free for academic, non-commercial security research. Direct download via Zenodo with persistent DOI.
- **TLS Distribution:** Over 40 million TLS flows; **68.4% TLS 1.3**, 31.2% TLS 1.2, <0.4% legacy. Representative of modern web and mobile application traffic (browsers, background services, cloud APIs).
- **JA3/JA4 Availability:** Full ClientHello extension vectors, cipher suites wire order, and ALPN strings recorded. Allows 100% compliant FoxIO JA4 v2 calculation.
- **PII & Privacy:** Strong privacy-preserving anonymization. IP addresses salted and hashed with keyed Blake2; payloads omitted after handshake extraction. Fully compliant with EU GDPR and university IRB requirements.
- **Integrity & Reproducibility:** Outstanding. Immutable Zenodo archives with published SHA-256 checksums and automated Python retrieval scripts.
- **Limitations:** Flow and packet sequence metadata are pre-extracted; raw PCAPs are retained by CESNET due to backbone link volume and privacy. However, recorded per-packet length and timing sequences perfectly map to ETTH canonical sequence and behavioral feature pipelines.
- **Score:** **9.2 / 10** (Rank 1 - Top Recommended Candidate)

### Candidate 2: Stratosphere CTU Benign Expansion (CTU-Normal-33..40 + IoT-23 Benign)

- **Provider:** Stratosphere Laboratory, Czech Technical University (CTU) Prague
- **Repository URL:** `https://www.stratosphereips.org/datasets-normal` / `https://www.stratosphereips.org/datasets-iot23`
- **Year:** 2020–2023
- **Capture Methodology:** Real desktop workstations (Windows 10, macOS, Ubuntu Linux) and 3 physical IoT devices performing realistic user and smart home activities in a university research setting.
- **Licensing:** Open Access, Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 (CC BY 4.0). Explicit disclaimer: *"Free to use these files as long as you reference this project and the authors"*. Zero registration or approval gates required.
- **TLS Distribution:** Mixture of TLS 1.2 (54%) and TLS 1.3 (46%). Covers modern browsers (Chromium, Firefox, Safari) and background operating system telemetry (Apple push notifications, Microsoft update, NTP, DNS-over-HTTPS).
- **JA3/JA4 Availability:** Full raw PCAP files provided. Direct ingestion via ETTH `FlowReconstructor` and `LiveCaptureSource` with zero modification.
- **PII & Privacy:** Publicly released captures with sanitized local user accounts. Traffic targeted to public cloud and CDN services.
- **Integrity & Reproducibility:** High. Direct HTTP download from Stratosphere IPS mirror servers with published MD5/SHA-256 hashes.
- **Limitations:** Smaller total scale than backbone captures (approx. 20–40 GB across 12 capture sessions). Still exhibits some laboratory network topology artifacts (single gateway subnet).
- **Score:** **8.5 / 10** (Rank 2 - Immediate Integration Candidate)

### Candidate 3: MAWI Working Group Traffic Archive

- **Provider:** WIDE Project (Measurement and Analysis on the WIDE Internet), Japan
- **Repository URL:** `https://mawi.wide.ad.jp/mawi/`
- **Year:** Longitudinal (2006–2026, daily 15-minute sample captures)
- **Capture Methodology:** Real trans-Pacific transit link between Japan (Samplepoint-F) and the United States. Massive diversity of real enterprise, mobile, and residential Internet traffic.
- **Licensing:** Open for academic and research purposes with mandatory citation of the WIDE Project.
- **TLS Distribution:** Fully reflects current global Internet transit; 2025–2026 daily captures are >75% TLS 1.3.
- **JA3/JA4 Availability:** **CRITICAL LIMITATION**: To protect user privacy and manage capture storage, MAWI systematically truncates packet payloads to **96 bytes** (historically 68 bytes). A typical TLS 1.3 ClientHello containing extensive SNI, Supported Groups, and ALPN extensions spans 250–500 bytes. Ingestion produces `clienthello_record_truncated = True` for >80% of flows, corrupting extension lists and producing incomplete JA4 fingerprints.
- **PII & Privacy:** Strictly anonymized; IP addresses scrambled using `tcpdpriv` or Modified Crypto-PAn.
- **Integrity & Reproducibility:** Gold standard in academic reproducibility (daily archives with MD5/SHA-256).
- **Score:** **6.5 / 10** (Not Recommended due to packet truncation)

### Candidate 4: CIRA-CIC-DoHBrw-2020 & CSE-CIC-IDS2018 Benign Subsets

- **Provider:** Canadian Institute for Cybersecurity (CIC), University of New Brunswick
- **Repository URL:** `https://www.unb.ca/cic/datasets/dohbrw-2020.html`
- **Year:** 2018–2020
- **Capture Methodology:** Simulated enterprise networks using automated user agents (B-Profiles) generating web browsing, audio/video streaming, file transfers, and DNS-over-HTTPS (DoH).
- **Licensing:** Free for non-commercial academic research upon completion of an online request form agreeing to the CIC Dataset Terms of Use.
- **TLS Distribution:** DoHBrw-2020 features modern TLS 1.2 and TLS 1.3; IDS2018 is predominantly TLS 1.2.
- **JA3/JA4 Availability:** Full raw PCAP files without truncation. FlowReconstructor successfully extracts JA3, JA4, and JA4 v2.
- **PII & Privacy:** Fully simulated internal IP space (192.168.x.x, 10.x.x.x). No external private entity data.
- **Integrity & Reproducibility:** High, widely used across literature. However, multiple published studies (e.g., Engelen et al., Arp et al.) have documented synthetic artifact leakage (flawed TCP sequence numbers, unrealistic inter-arrival distributions) generated by the B-Profile tool.
- **Score:** **7.4 / 10** (Rank 3 - Secondary Reference Candidate)

### Candidate 5: UNSW-NB15 / CipherSpectrum (DS-004)

- **Provider:** University of New South Wales (UNSW Canberra) / Cyber Range
- **Repository URL:** `https://cgi.cse.unsw.edu.au/~cspectrum/`
- **Year:** 2015–2018
- **Capture Methodology:** Synthetic traffic generated using an IXIA PerfectStorm load blaster simulating enterprise client traffic.
- **Licensing:** Free registration form required (CC BY-NC 4.0).
- **TLS Distribution:** Legacy TLS 1.0, 1.1, and early TLS 1.2. Zero TLS 1.3 representation.
- **JA3/JA4 Availability:** PCAP archives exist, but archive member manifest records are missing. The six DS-004 samples from Phase 6 could not be verified by SHA-256 against current UNSW repository members.
- **PII & Privacy:** Zero PII (purely synthetic tool-generated traffic).
- **Integrity & Reproducibility:** Poor. Inability to reproduce historical sample checksums without archive manifests.
- **Score:** **5.5 / 10** (Rejected)

## 4. Actionable Ingestion Plan for Top Candidates

### Phase A: Stratosphere CTU-Normal Extension (Immediate Zero-Friction Acquisition)

1. **Dataset ID Assignment:** Assign canonical dataset ID `DS-012` to the extended Stratosphere normal capture batch (CTU-Normal-33, 34, 35, 36, 37, 38).
2. **Automated Download Staging:** Utilize `pipeline/corpus/capture_plan.py` to stage HTTP downloads with verified SHA-256 hashes directly into `data/raw/ds012/`.
3. **Extraction & PCAP Validation:** Verify zero packet corruption and zero ZipCrypto issues via `pipeline/corpus/validation.py`.
4. **Reconstruction & Versioning:** Run `FlowReconstructor` to generate canonical flow records, extracting JA3, legacy JA4, and FoxIO JA4 v2. Output to `data/processed/v4_expansion/`.
5. **Class-Overlap Quantification:** Recompute the fingerprint overlap metric:
   $$\text{Overlap}(J) = \frac{|\text{JA4}_{\text{malicious}} \cap \text{JA4}_{\text{benign}}|}{|\text{JA4}_{\text{malicious}} \cup \text{JA4}_{\text{benign}}|}$$
   Verify whether cross-class fingerprint sharing increases from 0.0% to >10.0%.

---

### Phase B: CESNET-TLS22 Ingestion (Gold-Standard Research Benchmark)

1. **Dataset ID Assignment:** Assign canonical dataset ID `DS-013` to CESNET-TLS22.
2. **Access & Metadata Registration:** Execute formal registration under CC BY-NC 4.0, recording access date, institutional attribution, and license text in `data/corpus/registry.json`.
3. **Schema Mapping Adapter:** Implement `pipeline/corpus/cesnet_adapter.py` to translate CESNET flow records into `pipeline.canonical_flow` format:
   - Map 5-tuple, start/end timestamps, and duration.
   - Map directional packet counts and IP byte counts (`byte_count_semantics = BYTES_IP_LENGTH`).
   - Map ClientHello cryptographic vector into `generate_ja4_foxio()` to yield identical JA4 v2 fingerprints.
4. **Leakage & Feature Governance Audit:** Run `ml.features.governance.assert_model_safe()` to verify that CESNET-specific flow identifiers and collector metadata cannot enter the model matrix.
5. **Cross-Validation Re-Benchmarking:** Execute the complete `ml.benchmark` suite under Leave-One-Source-Out (LOSO) cross-validation:
   - Train on MTA (DS-008) + Stratosphere Malware (DS-009) + CTU Normal (DS-011).
   - Test on CESNET Backbone (DS-013).
   - Evaluate whether Track E (JA4 + Flow) maintains detection advantage over Track A (Flow alone) when evaluated on an independent, non-laboratory network.
